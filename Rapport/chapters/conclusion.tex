\chapter{Conclusion}

The graph isomorphism problem is a well-studied problem in graph theory, yet many aspects of it remain unresolved. Over the course of this thesis, we reviewed a significant portion of the most important research conducted on the topic, aiming to build a coherent overview of the field. Even so, a great deal about this problem remains to be discovered.

Regarding \Naut{} itself, the program remains remarkably impressive in its ability to solve GI.\@ Across every graph family we tested, its running time remained astonishingly low. Moreover, we only explored a fraction of what the program offers: \Naut{} includes a wide range of other features for working with graphs that fall outside the scope of this thesis. As long as no fundamentally more efficient practical approach to GI is discovered, \Naut{} is likely to remain a reference tool for solving it.

As for our own implementation, it remains slower than the original, though the gap stays reasonable rather than dramatic which is not entirely surprising given the decades of low-level optimisation behind McKay's implementation. Our comparison of pruning strategies confirmed that automorphism-based pruning is the most effective of the three across every graph family tested, consistently discarding the largest share of redundant branches. The comparison of the invariant methods was similarly informative: one of the three invariants proved considerably less discriminating than the other two, regardless of the graph family considered, making it the clearest candidate for improvement. Building a more discriminating replacement for it is a natural next step, together with testing the implementation on additional graph families to see whether the relative proportions of the different pruning strategies hold more generally.


In conclusion, the goal of this thesis was to reimplement \Naut{} entirely from scratch in C++. Despite our program not being as high-performing as the reference implementation, the process of studying \Naut{} in depth, understanding its internal mechanisms, and rebuilding it independently proved genuinely worthwhile in its own right. In that sense, the objective of this thesis has been successfully achieved.
